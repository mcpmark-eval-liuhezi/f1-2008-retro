# F1 2008 Season Dossier

Research packet for the 2008 Formula One retrospective issue — the split-titles year, when the drivers' and constructors' championships went to different teams.

## Season standings

**Constructors' Championship (final, 2008)**

- Winning team: Ferrari — 172 points (8 wins)
- Runner-up: McLaren — 151 points (6 wins)
- Gap between them: 21 points

**Drivers' Championship (final, 2008)**

- Champion: Lewis Hamilton, driving for McLaren — 98 points (5 wins)
- Runner-up: Felipe Massa (Ferrari) — 97 points (6 wins), just 1 point behind

All numbers pulled from live F1 season data (2008 constructor standings and driver standings, round 18).

## Book pick

This issue's book-review subject is Enzo Ferrari, founder of the 2008 constructors' champion.

- **Book:** *Enzo Ferrari* (subtitled *The Man, the Cars, the Races, the Machine*)
- **Author:** Brock Yates
- **First published:** 1991
- **Editions on Open Library:** 6 — the Enzo Ferrari biography with the most editions
- **Cover image:** https://covers.openlibrary.org/b/id/7146165-M.jpg

**About the author, Brock Yates**

- **Life dates:** October 21, 1933 – October 5, 2016
- **Short bio:** An American automotive journalist, author, screenwriter and TV commentator. He was the longtime executive editor of *Car and Driver* magazine and also wrote for *The Washington Post*, *Playboy* and *The American Spectator*. He co-conceived and ran the original Cannonball Run cross-country race, wrote the screenplay for the 1981 film *The Cannonball Run*, and authored more than a dozen books on cars and motor sport — his *Enzo Ferrari* is widely regarded as the definitive narrative of the Italian automaker's acerbic founder.
- **Author portrait:** https://covers.openlibrary.org/a/olid/OL2686681A-L.jpg
- **Open Library author page:** https://openlibrary.org/authors/OL2686681A

## Fact-check

**The claim readers correct every year:** how many constructors' championships has Ferrari won in total?

**Verified answer: 16 constructors' championships** — 1961, 1964, 1975, 1976, 1977, 1979, 1982, 1983, 1999, 2000, 2001, 2002, 2003, 2004, 2007 and 2008. The most recent of those is the 2008 title this issue celebrates, and the tally is the all-time record in Formula One.
